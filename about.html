Our Mission

Why We Built This

Tastes of Africa exists to celebrate, preserve, and share the extraordinary culinary heritage of the African continent. We believe that food is one of the most powerful ways to understand a culture — and Africa's food tells a story that the world deserves to hear.

Our mission is to make African recipes accessible to everyone: from the diaspora reconnecting with home flavours, to curious food lovers discovering a cuisine for the first time. We want every visitor to leave with a new ingredient to try, a new dish to cook, and a deeper appreciation for the continent's diversity.

History

A Brief History of African Food Culture

African cuisine is one of the oldest and most diverse in the world. Long before the spice trade connected Europe to Asia, African communities had developed sophisticated food systems rooted in agriculture, fermentation, and communal cooking.

In West Africa, the cultivation of sorghum, millet, and yams dates back thousands of years. The ancient kingdoms of Mali and Songhai were built on agricultural abundance. In North Africa, Berber and Arab influences merged to create the aromatic tagines and couscous dishes Morocco is famous for today.

East Africa's coastal Swahili culture absorbed flavours from Arab, Indian, and Persian traders — giving rise to dishes fragrant with cardamom, cloves, and coconut. In the Horn of Africa, Ethiopia developed a unique food culture centred on teff, a grain found nowhere else in the world.

Today, African cuisine is experiencing a global renaissance. Chefs across the continent and in the diaspora are reinterpreting traditional recipes, bringing them to international stages while honouring their roots.
