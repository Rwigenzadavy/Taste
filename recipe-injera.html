Injera

Injera is more than bread — it's the plate, the utensil, and the centrepiece of Ethiopian and Eritrean dining. Made from teff, an ancient grain native to the Horn of Africa, injera's spongy texture and sour flavour come from a natural fermentation process. Eating injera is a communal act: everyone tears from the same bread.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups teff flour (or a mix of teff and all-purpose flour)
  • 2½ cups water (plus more as needed)
  • ½ tsp salt
  • A pinch of baking soda (optional, for quicker rise)

Instructions

Show Cooking Instructions
  1. Mix teff flour and water in a bowl until smooth. Cover loosely with a cloth.
  2. Leave to ferment at room temperature for 2–3 days, stirring once daily. The batter should smell pleasantly sour and develop small bubbles.
  3. When ready, stir in salt. The batter should be thin — like crepe batter.
  4. Heat a non-stick pan or flat griddle over medium-high heat. Do not grease it.
  5. Pour a ladleful of batter in a spiral from the outside in, covering the pan. Tilt to spread if needed.
  6. Cover and cook for 2–3 minutes. Injera is done when the surface is dry and full of holes, and the edges lift naturally. Do not flip.
  7. Slide onto a clean cloth to cool. Serve with stews like doro wat or misir wat.
