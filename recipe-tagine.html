Moroccan Tagine

Named after the conical clay pot it's cooked in, tagine is the soul of Moroccan cuisine. The slow-cooking method traps steam and returns moisture to the dish, creating meltingly tender meat infused with cumin, cinnamon, and ras el hanout. It's a dish that tells the story of the ancient Silk Road trade routes.

Ingredients

  • 700g lamb shoulder, cut into chunks
  • 1 large onion, grated
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • ½ tsp ground ginger
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 100g dried apricots
  • 50g green olives
  • 1 preserved lemon, rind only, sliced
  • 400ml chicken stock
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Fresh coriander to garnish

Instructions

Show Cooking Instructions
  1. Mix all spices together. Coat the lamb pieces in the spice mix and leave to marinate for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Heat olive oil in a tagine or heavy casserole dish. Brown the lamb in batches and set aside.
  3. In the same pot, soften the grated onion and garlic for 5 minutes.
  4. Return the lamb to the pot. Add stock, apricots, olives, and preserved lemon.
  5. Bring to a simmer, then cover and cook on very low heat for 1.5–2 hours until the lamb is tender.
  6. Adjust seasoning and garnish with fresh coriander. Serve with couscous or crusty bread.
